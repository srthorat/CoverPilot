# Things worth querying in a proposed policy

## Red flag: grace period shorter than the rule
IRDAI's rule is a grace period of at least fifteen days for monthly premiums and thirty days for quarterly, half-yearly and yearly premiums, with no penalty and cover continuing. If the document gives fewer days, for example ten days for monthly, say it is a possible issue to confirm with the insurer, and cite the page.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 8; IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section IV, para 2.

## Red flag: suicide exclusion longer than twelve months
The minimum protection applies to death by suicide within twelve months of the start of cover or of a revival. If the document keeps the exclusion going for longer, such as twenty-four months, flag it as a possible issue and suggest asking the insurer why it differs from the twelve-month rule.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 21.

## Red flag: no refund or less than eighty percent on suicide
In the suicide window the nominee is entitled to at least eighty percent of the premiums paid, or the surrender value on the date of death, whichever is higher, as long as the policy is in force. A document that says no refund, or a lower percentage, is worth querying. Do not say what would happen to a particular claim.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 21.

## Red flag: free-look shorter than thirty days
The free-look period is thirty days from receiving the policy document, for policies with a term of one year or more. If the document or its covering letter gives fifteen days or another shorter period, ask the insurer which applies. Also check that the refund wording mentions only the proportionate risk premium, the medical examination cost and the stamp duty.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 5; IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section III, para 2.

## Red flag: contestability period is not three years
Under the Insurance Act and IRDAI's circular, a life policy cannot be questioned on any ground after three years from the latest of the issue date, the start of risk, the revival date or the rider date. A document that says two years, five years or no limit does not match, so flag it as a possible issue and ask the insurer to confirm.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 45; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 10.

## Red flag: no nomination section
Nomination is needed so the claim can be paid, and the policy should record the nominees, their shares and any appointee for a minor. If the document has no nomination section, say it is missing and suggest asking the insurer where the nominee details are recorded and how to confirm them.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 5.

## Red flag: sum assured different from your application
Compare the sum assured on the schedule with the one you chose. If they differ, such as one crore against one and a half crore, report it as a possible mismatch with both figures and the page, and suggest correcting the application or asking the insurer before paying.
Source: NexaSure Guardian check (document against application); IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 6.

## Red flag: premium different from your application
Compare the premium on the document with the one in your application, your quote or the illustration, whether it is higher or lower. A different amount can come from different riders, a different cover or an error. Report both figures and the page, say the cause is unknown, and suggest asking the insurer to explain the difference.
Source: NexaSure Guardian check (document against application).

## Red flag: term different from your application
The policy term on the schedule should match the term you applied for. If it does not, say so with both numbers and ask the insurer which one is correct, because the term decides how long the cover lasts.
Source: NexaSure Guardian check (document against application).

## Red flag: a rider you chose is not listed
If you chose a rider such as critical illness but the document does not list it, or lists it as excluded, report a possible omission and the page. Suggest asking whether the rider was left out by mistake and what premium and cover apply.
Source: NexaSure Guardian check (document against application); IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 1(B) (customers choose riders to suit their needs).

## Red flag: premium not stated as fixed for the term
For an individual policy, IRDAI's circular says the premium you choose at the outset must be level or uniform and must not vary over the premium paying term. Group products are the exception. A pure term plan may offer an option to lower the premium by up to fifty percent after three completed policy years, and once lowered it cannot be raised again. A document that says an individual term premium can be revised or will rise is worth querying.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, section IV part A, para 12 (page 11), read in the circular itself.

## Red flag: exclusions wider than the summary
Compare the exclusions in the policy wording with those in the Customer Information Sheet and the product summary. If the wording lists exclusions the summary did not mention, flag it as a possible difference and ask which prevails. The sheet is required to list the exclusions in simple words.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 2.

## Red flag: claim documents not listed
The insurer must state in the policy document which documents are needed to make a claim, and show the same on its website. If the document leaves the list out, ask for it. A claim cannot be rejected only because some documents are missing.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section V, paras 1 and 3.

## Red flag: a critical illness definition that looks different or thinner than usual
If the rider lists an illness whose definition seems narrower than the plain meaning, for example a cancer definition that excludes more than early or low-grade tumours, or a long required survival or lasting-symptom period, report a possible difference and the page, and do not call it wrong. IRDAI's 2020 health circular fixed standard wording for twenty-two critical illnesses for all insurers, but whether a critical illness rider on a term plan must use that exact wording could not be confirmed in the current texts. Suggest asking the insurer which definitions apply to this rider, whether they follow the IRDAI standard wording, and what the difference means for a claim, without saying whether any claim would be paid.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Standardization of Health Insurance Products, IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/193/07/2020, Chapter II (22 critical illnesses, definitions "without exception"); superseded by the Master Circular on Health Insurance Business 2024 (Annexure-6 items 1, 3, 33), which does not restate them; applicability to life riders not found in the life Master Circular 2024.

## Red flag: long waiting or survival periods on a rider
Riders such as critical illness often have a waiting period before cover starts and a survival period after diagnosis. IRDAI does not set a number for these on life riders: the insurer files them with the product, and the circular's product filing format lists waiting period and survival period as rider parameters the insurer states. So very long periods are worth asking about. Quote the numbers and the page, and ask what happens during the wait.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, filing format for riders, item 6.2(m) (waiting period, survival period); no IRDAI-prescribed period found in the circular or in the critical illness standardisation guidelines.

## Red flag: surrender or paid-up value promised on a pure term plan
Pure risk products do not acquire a surrender value, and policy loans are not available on them. If a pure term document promises a surrender value or a loan, ask the insurer to explain, because it is unusual for that kind of plan.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section IV, paras 3 and 4 and section V, para 3.

## Red flag: Customer Information Sheet or illustration missing
The Customer Information Sheet and the benefit illustration must be given with the policy. If either is missing from the pack, or the sheet has no Insurance Ombudsman contact details, ask the insurer to supply it.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, paras 2 and 3.

## Red flag: rider premium or benefit above IRDAI's limits
IRDAI limits riders. Critical illness or health rider premium cannot exceed one hundred percent of the base premium, and all other life riders together cannot exceed thirty percent. Each rider benefit cannot exceed the base sum assured, except an accidental death rider, up to three times. If the schedule shows riders above these limits, ask the insurer to explain.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 7.17.2 to 7.17.4 (pages 9 and 10), read in the circular itself.
