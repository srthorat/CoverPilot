# Reading a proposed policy document

## What a policy document package contains
A policy comes as a pack. The insurer must send it within fifteen days of accepting your proposal, at no extra charge: a covering letter that tells you about the free-look period, the policy document, a copy of the proposal form you submitted, a copy of the benefit illustration, the Customer Information Sheet, and any need-analysis document. If one of these is missing from what you were given, that is worth asking about.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 8.

## Insurer details and the UIN on a document
Every life insurance product and every rider has a unique identification number, and the insurer has to quote it in all the documents it gives you. Look for it on the schedule and on the Customer Information Sheet. A document with no such number, or a number that does not match the product name, is worth querying.
Source: IRDAI circular on filing of products and riders for life insurance business, IRDAI/ACTL/CIR/PRO/207/10/2022, 4 Oct 2022, chapter IV, para 44.

## How to read a UIN
This unique identification number has ten characters. The first three digits are the insurer's IRDAI registration number. The fourth is a letter for the kind of product: N for a non-linked product such as a pure term plan, L for unit-linked, B for a non-linked rider. The next three digits are the product's serial number, then the letter V, then two digits for the version, starting at 01. IRDAI publishes the list of cleared products on its website, so you can check the number there.
Source: IRDAI circular IRDAI/ACTL/CIR/PRO/207/10/2022, 4 Oct 2022, chapter IV, paras 45 and 46.

## Policy number versus proposal number
The proposal number is the reference of your application, before the insurer has decided. The policy number is given only when the insurer accepts the proposal and issues the policy. A draft or proposed document may show a proposal number and no policy number yet. That is normal, and it is not an issued policy or proof of cover.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, paras 6 and 7.

## The policy schedule
The schedule is the page with your specific details: the policyholder, the life assured, the sum assured, the policy term, the premium and how often it is paid, the due dates, and the nominee. The policyholder and the life assured can be the same person or different people. Check each line against what you entered in the application.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 6.

## Proposal date, issue date and start of cover
These are different dates. The proposal date is when you applied. The issue date is when the insurer issued the policy. Cover starts only after the insurer has accepted the proposal and received the premium. Section 45 counts its three years from the latest of the issue date, the date risk began, a revival date or a rider date, so it helps to know which dates the document shows.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 4 and section IV, para 6; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 10.

## Policy term versus premium paying term
The policy term is how long the cover lasts. The premium paying term is how long you pay. They can be the same, and a plan can let you pay for a shorter period than the cover lasts. A term plan can also run for as little as one month. Check that both numbers on the schedule match the plan you chose.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, paras 22 and 23.

## Sum assured and how it is paid
The sum assured is the amount paid on death while the policy is in force. The insurer may pay it as a lump sum or in instalments, depending on the terms of the policy, so look for the payout option the document describes. If the sum assured on the schedule is different from the one you chose, that is a possible mismatch to raise.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 20.

## Premium, payment mode and due dates
The premium table shows the amount, whether you pay monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly, and the due date. The payment mode matters because the grace period is fifteen days for monthly payments and thirty days for the other modes. Premium is paid only to the insurer's account, directly or through its authorised channel, and the insurer must give a valid acknowledgement immediately.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section IV, paras 1 and 2.

## How GST appears on life premiums
Individual life insurance premiums are exempt from GST from 22 September 2025, so a premium table should not add an eighteen percent line. If the document shows GST on a term plan for an individual, ask whether it applies to that policy. Older documents may still show it.
Source: GST Council 56th meeting press release, 3 Sep 2025.

## The riders table
If you chose riders, the document should list each one with its cover amount, its own premium, and its limits and exclusions. A rider has its own identification number and its own date, and that date also counts for Section 45. A rider you chose that does not appear in the table is a possible omission to raise before you pay.
Source: IRDAI circular IRDAI/ACTL/CIR/PRO/207/10/2022, chapter IV; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 10.

## Nominee and appointee on the document
Nomination is required so that the claim can be paid. You may name more than one nominee and set a percentage for each, and the shares should add up to one hundred percent. If a nominee is a minor, an adult appointee is named to receive the money until the child grows up. Nomination can be changed at any time during the policy. A document with no nomination section at all is worth querying.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 5; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 6.

## The Customer Information Sheet
The Customer Information Sheet is a plain-language summary that comes with every policy. It states the type of insurance, the sum assured, the benefits, any exclusions, the free-look period, revival, policy loan and other options, the claims procedure, policy servicing and grievance redressal, with the contact details of the Insurance Ombudsman for your area. The insurer must get your acknowledgement of it, and you can ask for it in your local language. If it says something different from the policy wording, ask which one applies.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, section IX and para 2.

## The benefit illustration
The insurer must give a customised benefit illustration at the point of sale. It is signed by both you and the person who sold the policy, and it forms part of the policy document. For products that have a surrender value it shows the values year by year. For a pure term plan, use it to check the sum assured, the premium and the term against the schedule.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 3 and para 26.5.
