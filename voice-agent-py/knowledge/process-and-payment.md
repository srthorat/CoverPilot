# Buying process, KYC, payment and e-sign

## Steps to buy a policy
You fill in your details, choose the cover amount and riders, name your nominees, review the summary, then pay and e-sign. At the end the policy document is issued. You can ask the assistant to explain any step or to fill a field for you. The steps in this demo are a NexaSure design, not an IRDAI sequence.
Source: NexaSure app behaviour (not a regulation).

## KYC documents needed
Insurers must verify your identity, your address and a recent photograph before issuing a policy. They may do it by Aadhaar online authentication, Aadhaar offline verification, digital KYC, a recorded live video check, a KYC identifier from the central KYC registry, or officially valid documents, and in addition they ask for your PAN or a declaration (Form 97, which replaced Form 60 from 1 April 2026) where it applies, and anything else the insurer requires. The officially valid documents are the passport, the driving licence, proof of possession of an Aadhaar number, the voter's identity card, the NREGA job card, or a letter from the Unique Identification Authority of India or the National Population Register that shows your name, address and Aadhaar number, plus any other document the government notifies. PAN is asked for separately; it is not one of those documents. If your document has no address, a recent utility bill, a property tax receipt, a bank statement, a pension payment order or an employer's accommodation letter can show your address. Aadhaar is not forced: if you cannot or do not want to use Aadhaar authentication you can give officially valid documents instead. In a video check the insurer must black out your Aadhaar number. Your name and date of birth must match the document exactly.
Source: IRDAI Master Guidelines on AML/CFT, 1 Aug 2022, paras 8.1.6, 8.1.7 and Annexure II (video identification, para p); Prevention of Money-laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules 2005, rule 2(1)(d) (officially valid documents), rule 9(4) (PAN or Form 60) and rule 9(18), read on https://fiuindia.gov.in/files/AML_Legislation/notification.html (a consolidated copy; the Gazette governs).

## Paying the premium
You can pay yearly, half-yearly, quarterly or monthly. Common ways are UPI, net banking, debit or credit card and auto-debit. Paying yearly is usually cheaper overall than monthly. In this demo no real money is charged. Cash payments are limited by anti-money-laundering rules.
Source: IRDAI Master Guidelines on AML/CFT 2022; premium modes are standard practice, NexaSure payment is a demo.

## Electronic signature and consent
An e-signature uses an Aadhaar OTP or a similar licensed method, run by a licensed provider under the Controller of Certifying Authorities. A valid e-signature has the same legal force as a handwritten signature. Before you sign, read the declaration, because it makes your answers the basis of the policy.
Source: Information Technology Act 2000 and Controller of Certifying Authorities eSign, https://cca.gov.in/eSign.html

## GST on the premium
Individual life insurance policies, including term plans, have been exempt from GST since 22 September 2025. Before that date premiums carried eighteen percent GST. Group policies and riders may be treated differently, so check your policy document.
Source: GST Council 56th meeting press release, 3 Sep 2025.

## Policy document and free look
After payment you receive the policy document by email, and often by post. Read it within thirty days of receipt. If you do not like it you can cancel in that free-look period.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 5.

## Increasing or changing cover after buying
Cover is fixed at issue. Extra cover needs a new proposal with fresh underwriting, apart from a life stage rider that raises cover on marriage or a child. In this demo, that rider is a NexaSure design.
Source: NexaSure demo product terms (not a regulation).
