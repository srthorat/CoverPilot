# Nomination and trust allocation

## Nominee eligibility
Under Section 39 of the Insurance Act, you can nominate any person. But only your parents, spouse and children count as beneficial nominees, who keep the money for themselves. Anyone else, such as a sibling or friend, receives it only as a collector, and your legal heirs can still claim it. For certainty, name your spouse, children or parents. This is a legal rule.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 39, https://lawgist.in/insurance-act/39; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 6.

## Multiple nominees and shares
You can name more than one nominee and give each a percentage share. The shares must add up to exactly one hundred percent. If one nominee dies before you, that share goes to the surviving nominees in proportion, unless you have said otherwise. Name the nominees, shares and relationships clearly in the form.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 39; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 6.

## Adding a second child or another nominee
To add a second child, add them as another nominee in the nomination step and split the shares so the total stays one hundred percent. For example, fifty percent each. If the child is under eighteen, you must also name an adult appointee. You can edit the nominee list at any time later.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 39; NexaSure form behaviour (not a regulation).

## Minor nominees and appointees
If a nominee is under eighteen, you must name an adult appointee to receive the money on the child's behalf until they turn eighteen. A grandparent or the other parent is a common choice. The appointee holds the money for the child and cannot use it as their own.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 39(3); IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 6.

## Changing or cancelling a nomination
You can change or cancel a nomination at any time before the policy matures or you die, through a written endorsement the insurer registers. A new nomination replaces the old one. If you assign the policy to someone, for example to a lender, the nomination is cancelled to the extent of that assignment.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 39(2) and section 38; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 6.

## Married Women's Property Act 1874, Section 6 trust
If you take the policy under Section 6 of the Married Women's Property Act, it becomes a trust for your wife and children. The money is generally protected from your creditors and cannot be reached by a court decree against you. Once created you cannot change the beneficiaries or assign the policy. The wife and children must be named as the beneficiaries when the policy is issued. This is a legal rule, and creditor protection can still be argued in court, so ask a lawyer.
Source: Married Women's Property Act 1874 section 6; Insurance Act 1938 section 39(12).

## Recommended split example
One common split is seventy percent to the spouse and thirty percent to a child, with an adult appointee for the child. It is only an example, not advice. You decide the split, and the shares must total one hundred percent.
Source: NexaSure demo example (not a regulation).
