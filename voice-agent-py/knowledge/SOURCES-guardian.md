# VERIFIED against primary text on 30 Oct 2026 (downloaded, pdftotext, paragraph read):
# Level premium for an individual policy: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, section IV part A, para 12 (page 11): the premium chosen at the outset is payable throughout the premium paying term and shall be level or uniform and not vary (group products excepted); an option to lower it by up to 50 percent is allowed after three completed policy years for pure risk products and once lowered it cannot be raised.
# Rider limits: same circular, para 7.17.2 to 7.17.4: health or critical illness rider premium at most 100 percent of the base premium, all other life riders together at most 30 percent, benefit per rider at most the base sum assured (accidental death rider up to three times), riders take the nature of the base policy.
# Rider waiting and survival periods: no IRDAI-prescribed period exists in the life products circular or in the Guidelines on Standardization in Health Insurance 2016 (https://irdai.gov.in/documents/38105/48616/Guidelines+on+Standardization+in+Health+Insurance+2016.pdf/2ed4f16d-2eb3-6a85-0dba-41d71d9e9064?version=1.0&t=1631522672416&download=true), which only fix the definitions of 22 critical illnesses (chapter II, to be used without exception); the life circular's rider filing format item 6.2(m) lists waiting period and survival period as parameters the insurer states. Some definitions carry their own condition (stroke: permanent neurological deficit assessed at least 30 days after the event).
# Riders on lapse: no rule found for non-linked riders. IRDAI (Insurance Products) Regulations 2024, Schedule I clause 1.10 defines revival as restoration with or without rider benefits. For unit-linked products the life circular para 40.1.1 (rider cover ceases on discontinuance) and 40.1.7.2.1 (rider may be revived at the policyholder's option) apply.
# Surrender and paid-up on pure term: Regulations 2024, Schedule I clause 4(A)(a) (guaranteed surrender value for non-linked products other than pure risk products) and 4(A)(a)(6) and (7) (paid-up rules for policies that acquired a surrender value); life circular para 7.12 (limited or single pay pure risk products: non-forfeiture benefit reasonable and fair). Official text: https://irdai.gov.in/web/guest/document-detail?documentId=4590475 (Gazette of India Extraordinary, 20 March 2024).
# Revival period: Regulations 2024, Schedule I clause 1.39 (under C, definitions applicable to non-linked insurance products): five consecutive complete years from the date of first unpaid premium; clause 1.28 (under B, linked): three years. The policy document states how revival works (PPHI circular section III para 3).
# Claim rejection: PPHI circular 2024 section IV para 6 (no repudiation without legally tenable evidence) and section V para 1(ii) (no rejection or closure for want of documents or delayed intimation); the requirement to give full details with reference to the specific policy terms is in the health claims paragraph 5 and is for health claims only. Insurance Act 1938 section 45(2) and (4) and their provisos, read in the Gazette copy of the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act 2015 (Act No. 5 of 2015, 20 March 2015): https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_parliament/2015/the-insurance-laws-(amendment)-act,-2015.pdf : grounds and materials in writing, premiums returned within 90 days for a misstatement that is not fraud.
# Ombudsman fifty lakh limit: G.S.R. 828(E), 9 November 2023, amendment to rule 17(3) proviso clause (ii), 'thirty' replaced by 'fifty', read in the Gazette copy (https://www.cioins.co.in/notification/Insurance%20Ombudsman(Amendment)%20Rules,%202023.pdf).
