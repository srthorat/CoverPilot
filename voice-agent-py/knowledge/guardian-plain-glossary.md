# Policy words in plain language

## Plain meaning of indemnity and why it does not apply to term life
Indemnity means the insurer repays a loss up to its actual size, as in a health or motor claim. A term life policy does not work that way. It pays a fixed sum assured on death, whatever the family's actual loss. So you will not find the loss-matching rules that appear in health documents.
Source: General insurance principle; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 20.

## Plain meaning of misrepresentation
Misrepresentation means a statement in the proposal that was wrong. If a wrong statement or a hidden fact matters to the life expectancy, the insurer may question the policy within the first three years, and it must give its grounds in writing. After three years the policy cannot be questioned on any ground.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 45.

## Plain meaning of material fact
A material fact is a fact that matters to how the insurer assesses the risk, such as health, age, occupation or habits like smoking. Not mentioning something that is not material does not by itself make a statement false. Silence about a fact only counts if there was a duty to speak about it.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 45; IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section II, para 3.

## Plain meaning of repudiation
Repudiation is the insurer refusing a claim, or refusing to honour the policy. It needs legally tenable evidence. A document that talks about repudiation is describing when the insurer may refuse, which is why the Guardian explains the clause and does not predict what will happen.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section IV, para 6.

## Plain meaning of contestability
Contestability is the period during which the insurer can question the policy. By law and by IRDAI's circular it is three years from the latest of the issue date, the start of risk, a revival date or a rider date. After that, no ground is enough.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 45; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 10.

## Plain meaning of free-look
Free-look is the thirty days after you receive the policy in which you can read it and cancel if you do not agree with any term. The insurer must accept the request whatever your reason, and refunds the premium less a proportionate risk premium, any medical examination cost and the stamp duty.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section III, para 2.

## Plain meaning of grace period
The grace period is the extra time after the due date to pay without any penalty or late fee: fifteen days for monthly premiums and thirty days for the other modes. The policy stays in force and the cover continues during it.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 8.

## Plain meaning of lapse and revival
A policy lapses when the premium is not paid by the end of the grace period. Revival is bringing a lapsed policy back by paying the unpaid premiums with interest or a late fee, possibly with a health declaration or medical examination, within the period the document gives.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, section III, para 3.

## Plain meaning of rider
A rider is an optional extra benefit attached to the base policy for its own premium, for example critical illness, accidental death or waiver of premium. It has its own cover amount, limits and exclusions, and its own date.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 1(B); IRDAI circular IRDAI/ACTL/CIR/PRO/207/10/2022, chapter IV.

## Plain meaning of exclusion
An exclusion is something the policy does not pay for, or pays for only under limits, such as death by suicide in the first twelve months, which is treated by a minimum rule. Exclusions should be listed in the policy and summarised in the Customer Information Sheet.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, paras 2 and 21.

## Plain meaning of waiting period and survival period
A waiting period is a time after the policy starts during which a benefit, usually a rider, is not payable. A survival period is the time a person must live after a diagnosis before a rider pays. IRDAI does not prescribe the number of days for life riders; the insurer states them in the product, so read the numbers in the document and ask if they are unclear. Some definitions carry their own conditions, such as a stroke needing a lasting deficit assessed at least thirty days after the event.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024 (IRDAI/ACTL/MSTCIR/MISC/89/6/2024, 12 Jun 2024), rider filing format item 6.2(m): "Any other parameter specific to the rider [like waiting period, survival period, group sizes etc.]", which the insurer fills in; no IRDAI-prescribed number of days found in that circular.

## Plain meaning of critical illness definitions in a rider
A critical illness rider pays a lump sum when a listed illness is diagnosed, and the exact meaning of each illness is written in the rider wording. IRDAI's health circular of 2020 fixed standard wording for twenty-two critical illnesses and said all insurers must use it without exception wherever a product covers one of those illnesses; it does not say how many illnesses a product must cover. In May 2022 IRDAI changed three of them with immediate effect: cancer of specified severity, multiple sclerosis with persisting symptoms, and loss of speech (which must last twelve continuous months). The 2024 Master Circular on Health Insurance Business later listed the 2020 circular and the 2022 change as superseded and does not repeat the definitions. I could not find a current IRDAI text that says in so many words that a critical illness rider on a term plan must use that wording, and I could not find one that says it does not. So I cannot tell you that your rider must match it. Read the definition printed in your own rider, compare the key conditions, and ask the insurer which definitions it uses and why any differ.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Standardization of Health Insurance Products, IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/193/07/2020, 22 Jul 2020, Chapter II: "The following nomenclature and procedure are being prescribed for 22 critical illnesses that could form part of a health insurance policy. All Insurers shall use the definitions without exception wherever the products offer coverage to any of the Critical Illnesses specified herein." (read on irdai.gov.in); IRDAI circular IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/104/5/2022, 27 May 2022 (clauses 1, 11 and 18 substituted); IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business, IRDAI/HLT/CIR/PRO/84/5/2024, 29 May 2024, Part IV: "This Circular supersedes all the Guidelines/Circulars listed in Annexure-6" (items 1, 3 and 33 are the 2020 circular, its corrigendum and the 2022 change); IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, para 91.4 (the form for a health product or health rider offered by a life insurer follows the health circular), para 7.17.2 (premium of "health related or critical illness riders" at most 100 percent of the base premium) and para 63.8.5 (critical illness rider listed as a rider type). Whether a critical illness rider is bound by the standard wording is not settled by these texts.

## Plain meaning of nominee, beneficiary and assignee
A nominee is the person you name to receive the money on your death. A beneficiary nominee keeps it for themselves, and that is true of your parents, spouse and children. Other nominees receive it as collectors for your legal heirs. An assignee is someone, such as a lender, to whom you transfer rights in the policy by endorsement.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 sections 38 and 39; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, paras 6 and 7.

## Plain meaning of appointee and the Married Women's Property Act trust
An appointee is the adult who receives the money for a nominee who is under eighteen until that child grows up. A policy taken under section 6 of the Married Women's Property Act is held in trust for the wife and children, which generally keeps the money out of the husband's creditors' reach.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 39; Married Women's Property Act 1874 section 6.

## Plain meaning of UIN, endorsement and benefit illustration
The unique identification number, or U I N, is the product's registered number. An endorsement is a written change added to the policy, such as a nominee change. A benefit illustration is the customised sheet showing the sum assured, premium and any benefit values, signed by you and the seller at the time of sale.
Source: IRDAI circular IRDAI/ACTL/CIR/PRO/207/10/2022, chapter IV; IRDAI Master Circular on Life Insurance Products 2024, paras 3 and 7.
