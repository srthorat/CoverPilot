# Claims, grievances and the insurance ombudsman

## Documents for a death claim
The nominee sends the claim form, the original policy or its details, the death certificate, the nominee's ID and bank proof. If the death was unnatural, a police report and post-mortem may be needed. The insurer may ask for more, but cannot reject a claim only because some documents are missing.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, life section, "At the time of claim".

## How long a claim takes
IRDAI requires a death claim to be settled within fifteen days of getting all documents. If the insurer needs to investigate, it must finish within forty-five days. If it pays late, it owes interest at the bank rate plus two percent. NexaSure's two-hour response is a demo target.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, life section, claim timelines.

## Why claims get rejected
The main reasons are a wrong or hidden fact in the proposal within the first three years, a lapsed policy, and a death within an excluded period. After three years a claim can be rejected only for proven fraud. The insurer must give its reasons in writing.
Source: Insurance Act 1938 section 45; IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024.

## Making a complaint
First write to the insurer's grievance officer. The insurer must acknowledge the complaint immediately and give a resolution within fourteen days, with reasons and the policy terms if it does not accept it. If you are not satisfied, escalate to IRDAI through its grievance portal, Bima Bharosa, or call its toll-free helpline. Keep the complaint number safe.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, grievance redressal; IRDAI Bima Bharosa portal.

## Insurance ombudsman
First complain to the insurer in writing or online. If it rejects the complaint, does not reply within one month, or you are not satisfied with the reply, you can go to the Insurance Ombudsman within one year of the rejection or reply (or one month after you complained, if there was no reply). It is free and you do not need a lawyer. The Ombudsman must pass an award within three months of getting everything needed, and the insurer must comply within thirty days. The award cannot exceed the loss suffered, and is capped at fifty lakh rupees including expenses. That cap was thirty lakh until an amendment in November 2023, and some older pages still show thirty lakh. You cannot use the Ombudsman if the same matter is already before a court, consumer forum or arbitrator, but you can go to a consumer court instead.
Source: Insurance Ombudsman Rules 2017 rules 14, 16, 17 (consolidated to 18 May 2021); the fifty lakh cap is Rule 17(3) proviso (ii) as amended by G.S.R. 828(E) of 9 November 2023, read in the Gazette copy (https://www.cioins.co.in/notification/Insurance%20Ombudsman(Amendment)%20Rules,%202023.pdf); a draft of further changes, G.S.R. 864(E) of 25 November 2025, was published for comment and was not confirmed as final; free and no lawyer: IRDAI FAQ on the Ombudsman Rules.

## Unclaimed amounts
If a claim or refund stays unpaid, the insurer must still hold it and show it on its website. An unclaimed amount is money unpaid twelve months after its due date because the person could not be reached. You can search for it on IRDAI's Bima Bharosa site, matching any two of policy number, PAN, name and date of birth. If it stays unclaimed for ten years the insurer moves it to the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund, and you can still claim it through the insurer for up to twenty-five years after that. Keep your mobile number, address and bank details up to date so claims are not delayed.
Source: IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024, life section, unclaimed amount para.
