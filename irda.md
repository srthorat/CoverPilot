For CoverPilot v2.0, I would design the product around an Indian individual term-life insurance application and its policy documents, following applicable IRDAI requirements.

The important distinction is that the insurance application form, the policy document, and the policy brochure are three different things. CoverPilot should help the customer understand all three, verify information consistently, and explain relevant regulatory requirements without pretending every insurer uses identical wording.

A good starting point is to organize CoverPilot around the customer's journey: before buying, during application, while reviewing the policy, and when making a claim.
1. What should be included in an Indian term insurance policy document?
The following is a practical checklist for CoverPilot. Exact wording and applicable clauses depend on the insurer's approved product and policy terms.
A. Policy identity and coverage
Section	Information CoverPilot should identify
Insurer details	Legal insurer name, contact details, customer support and grievance channels
Product identity	Product name, Unique Identification Number (UIN), policy number
Parties	Policyholder, life assured, nominee and relevant relationships
Policy schedule	Issue date, commencement date, policy term, maturity/expiry date where applicable
Sum assured	Death benefit and any other benefits provided under the product
Premium details	Premium amount, payment frequency, premium-paying term, due dates and applicable charges/taxes
Benefit structure	Level or increasing cover, payout options, return-of-premium option if offered, and riders selected

The IRDAI Life Insurance Products Master Circular contains product-governance requirements, benefit illustration requirements, and a prescribed Customer Information Sheet format. 
IRDAI+1

B. Benefits, exclusions and policy conditions
Section	What to explain
Death benefit	What is payable, to whom, and under which conditions
Rider benefits	Critical illness, accidental death or other riders actually attached to the policy
Exclusions	All applicable exclusions, including product-specific exclusions
Suicide clause	Applicable conditions and benefit treatment under the actual policy wording
Premium grace period	Deadline and consequences when a premium is late
Lapse and revival	When cover may lapse and the conditions for revival
Policy termination	Expiry, cancellation and other applicable termination conditions
Free-look period	Applicable review/cancellation rights and refund deductions
Claim process	Required documents, notification process, claim assessment and payment conditions
Nomination and assignment	Applicable rights and procedures for changing or recording a nominee or assigning the policy

IRDAI's 2024 policyholder-protection master circular addresses exclusions, policy conditions, and the Customer Information Sheet. The actual policy must state the exclusions applicable to its product. 
caalley.com+1

C. Rights, disclosures and support
CoverPilot should also find and explain:
- Proposal and declarations: The information the customer supplied and the importance of truthful, complete disclosure.
- Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938: Explain the statutory rules governing when a life policy can be questioned, including the three-year provision and its legal conditions. Do not describe this as an unconditional guarantee that every claim will be paid after three years.
- Grievance redressal: Insurer complaint channels, escalation procedures and the applicable Insurance Ombudsman contact.
- Customer Information Sheet (CIS): A plain-language summary of the policy, including benefits, exclusions, free-look period, claim procedure and grievance information.
- Benefit illustration: The applicable illustration showing the product's benefits and costs, where required.
IRDAI's 2024 policyholder-protection master circular specifies the documents to be provided with an accepted life policy, including the policy document, proposal form, benefit illustration and CIS. 
caalley.com+1

2. Documents CoverPilot should process separately
Do not combine everything into one generic "policy PDF" category.
Policy document

The issued contract, policy schedule, applicable terms, clauses and endorsements.



Proposal/application form

Personal details, occupation, income, health history, lifestyle disclosures, nominee details and declarations.



Customer Information Sheet (CIS)

The prescribed plain-language policy summary, useful for quick explanations and checks.



Benefit illustration and product brochure

The benefit illustration supports understanding of the proposed benefits and costs; the brochure explains the product before purchase.



Supporting documents

Identity/address proofs, income documents, medical records and insurer-requested evidence, processed only when needed and with appropriate consent.



IRDAI's official Life Insurance Products portal provides product-specific policy documents and terms, which is a useful source for building a grounded CoverPilot knowledge base. 
IRDAI+1

3. How CoverPilot should use this checklist
For each policy product, build a structured knowledge record containing:
- Product name, insurer and UIN.
- Policy-document version and effective date.
- Clause identifier, exact text and page number.
- Plain-language explanation, clearly separated from the original wording.
- Applicability conditions and exceptions.
- Related CIS, proposal form and benefit illustration.
- Source URL and last verification date.
Then the agent can answer questions such as “What happens if I miss a premium?”, highlight the exact clause, explain it in Marathi, and distinguish what the policy explicitly states from what remains uncertain.

Important: This checklist is a product-design guide, not a certification that a policy is IRDAI-compliant. For the hackathon, use an actual insurer's published term-insurance documents or a clearly labelled fictional policy. For a production product, verify the exact requirements against the current IRDAI regulations and the insurer's applicable policy wording.

3. Clauses that should not be forced into every term policy
Some policy clauses are conditional, not universal:
- Bonuses and participation in profits.
- Fund choices, switching and investment charges for linked products.
- Partial withdrawals, top-ups and maturity benefits.
- Guaranteed surrender value or policy loans.
- Conversion to paid-up status.
- Rider-specific exclusions and benefits.
- Special payout or settlement options.
For a pure term-life policy, many of these may be inapplicable. CoverPilot should report “Not applicable to this product” rather than flagging a missing clause when the feature is not offered.
4. Recommended implementation for CoverPilot
Treat the 55 items above as a product-review checklist, not as 55 clauses that must literally appear in every policy.

For each actual policy, store:
- Clause ID and clause heading.
- Exact source text and page number.
- Whether it is mandatory, product-specific or conditional.
- Applicable product name and UIN.
- Effective version/date of the source.
- Plain-language explanation in English, Hindi and Marathi.
- Validation result: present, missing, unclear, inconsistent or not applicable.
- Source citation and confidence.
The source of truth should be the applicable IRDAI regulation or circular and the insurer's actual product document—not an LLM's recollection of standard insurance wording.
